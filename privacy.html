Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 10, 2026

FullVision is a gradebook and learning-profile tool for teachers in British Columbia. This page explains what information FullVision stores, where it is kept, who can see it, and how it is deleted.

Who runs FullVision

FullVision is built and run by Colin Brown, a teacher in British Columbia. It is an independent tool. It is not run by a school district or the Ministry of Education and Child Care. Questions about privacy go to colin.brown04@gmail.com.

Who uses it

Only teachers have accounts. Students and parents do not, and FullVision never emails or messages them. A student opens their own page with a printed card, or a questionnaire or a report with a slip, as described below. Teachers enter student information themselves, so each teacher decides what goes in. Before entering student information, a teacher should confirm that their school district allows it.

Student questionnaires

A teacher can turn on short questionnaires, such as About Me or a Core Competency reflection, and print a slip for each student. The student scans the slip's code with a phone and answers there, without an account.

  • The phone shows only the student's first name, the class name and the questions. It never shows marks, designations, notes or another student's information.
  • Each code works once, only for that student, and only while the questionnaire is open. FullVision stores a slip's code scrambled (as a hash), not as printed. A slip's code is short, so someone with a copy of the database could work the codes out from it.
  • Before the questions open, the student shows the slip is theirs: their birthday or student number, checked against what the teacher entered, or, for a student with neither, a four-digit password FullVision prints on the slip and keeps, as printed, with the student's record. After five wrong tries the slip stops working.
  • The answers go to the teacher's gradebook and nowhere else. A question the teacher marks as private, such as "Is there anything else you want me to know?", is seen only by the teacher and never printed on a report.
  • To stop people guessing codes, FullVision blocks a network after repeated wrong codes. For this it keeps a scrambled form of the phone's network address with each attempt for 30 days. The address itself is not stored.

Reports on students' phones

A teacher can send a student's progress report to the student's phone. The student opens it with the code on their slip and their birthday. The report is for reading; there is nothing to send back.

  • The phone shows a copy the teacher made when they sent it: the student's levels, the teacher's comment, the student's own goal and self-assessment, and attendance. It never shows designations, notes, observations or private answers. The teacher can update the copy or close the link at any time.
  • The link works until the date the teacher sets. FullVision checks the birthday against the date of birth the teacher entered. After five wrong birthdays the page stops; scanning the code again starts over, but the link takes no more than ten wrong birthdays in 15 minutes. Students can open their report as often as they like.

Student cards

A teacher can turn on a Student Page for a class and print a card for each student. The student scans or types the card's code on a phone or Chromebook and opens their own page for that class, without an account.

  • The page shows the student's first name, the class, and the work the teacher has returned: each result, the teacher's comments and the rubric, and marks such as Missing or Late. It never shows a grade the teacher has not returned, designations, notes, observations or another student's information.
  • Before the page opens, the student shows the card is theirs: their birthday or student number, checked against what the teacher entered, or, for a student with neither, a four-digit password printed on the card. A card's password is its own, not the one on the student's slips. Five wrong tries in a row, or 15 in a week, lock the card until the teacher unlocks it.
  • FullVision does not keep a card's code or its password. The database keeps only a keyed fingerprint of each: a value made from the code with a secret key. The key is kept in the settings of the two Supabase services that make and check cards, never in the database, and the operator keeps one copy so a lost key does not mean reprinting every card. A fingerprint cannot be turned back into a code, and without the key it cannot be used to test a guess, so a copy of the database gives nobody a working card.
  • The code is printed once, on the card. No teacher's screen shows it, and nobody can look it up after printing. So the sheet can be printed again, the teacher's browser holds the codes in memory until the teacher leaves the screen, for ten minutes at most, and never saves them. A lost card is replaced: the teacher prints a new card with a new code, and the old card stops working.
  • A card works until the class's last reporting date, or a day the teacher chooses. It stops sooner if the teacher prints a new card, turns the Student Page off, withdraws the student or deletes the class. A device stays signed in until midnight, until the student taps Done, or until the page has gone 15 minutes unused.
  • For each card FullVision records its card number, when it was made, printed and last opened, its wrong tries, whether it is locked or replaced, and the last day it works. The teacher sees these in Student Cards. Each try at a code is logged with the time and the result, with the same scrambled network address as for slips, and kept for 30 days.
  • When a student opens a returned piece of work, FullVision records the time, so the page can mark the grade Updated if the teacher changes it later. Teachers do not see this time.

What FullVision stores

About Information
Teachers Email address, display name, password (stored only as a one-way hash), and display settings. The sign-in service also records the time and IP address of each sign-in. A teacher who uses Continue with Google has no FullVision password; Google shares their name, email address and profile picture.
Students Name, preferred name, pronouns, student number, email address, date of birth, learning designations (such as an IEP), and parents' or guardians' email addresses. Only a first name is required; the teacher chooses whether to add the rest. For a student with no birthday or student number, the questionnaire slip password.
Learning records Scores, proficiency levels and comments on assignments; observations; notes; goals and reflections; attendance; term ratings and report-card comments; and flags the teacher sets.
Student answers What students write in their teacher's questionnaires and in replies to their reports, and their Core Competency self-assessments. Each answer is saved with the time it was sent.
Student cards For each card, a keyed fingerprint of its code and, for a student with no birthday or student number, of its password; its card number; when it was made, printed and last opened; wrong tries; and the last day it works. Never the code or the password itself. When the student opened each returned piece of work.
Reports sent to phones The copy of each student's report the teacher sent to phones, and when the student opened it.
Classes Class names, competencies, assignments, rubrics and categories the teacher creates.
Change history When a score or term rating changes, the old and new values and the time of the change.

FullVision does not use advertising, analytics or tracking cookies. It does not sell, rent or share information for marketing. It does not send student information to artificial-intelligence services.

Where it is stored

  • The database is in Canada. All teacher and student information is stored by Supabase in its Montreal data centre (AWS ca-central-1).
  • Student answers and student cards go between the student's device and a Supabase service that runs in the same Montreal data centre, and from there to the database. Cards are made by a second service in the same place. Both refuse to run anywhere else. Neither passes through Netlify.
  • Web pages are delivered by Netlify, which serves them from data centres around the world. Netlify handles page requests, including your IP address, but does not receive the information in your gradebook.
  • Error reports go to Sentry, which stores them in Germany. When the app hits an error, it sends the error message, where in the code it happened, the page, and the browser type. Before a report is sent, FullVision removes the app's log messages, form contents and the part of the page address that identifies a student, and masks email addresses, so reports do not carry student information.
  • Your own device. FullVision keeps a copy of your classes in your browser so it works offline, along with changes waiting to be saved. Signing out removes this copy. On a shared computer, always sign out.

Who can see it

  • You. Each teacher sees only their own classes and students. The database enforces this on every request; signed-in teachers cannot read another teacher's data, and people who are not signed in cannot read anything but their own: a student can answer a questionnaire, or open their own report, with the code on their slip, and open their own page with their card.
  • The operator. Colin Brown has administrator access to the database. He uses it only to keep FullVision running and to fix problems, such as a save that failed, and does not look at student information for any other reason. The database holds no card code for him to see.
  • Service providers. Supabase, Netlify and Sentry run the systems described above. They process information only to provide their service to FullVision. Google signs in teachers who choose Continue with Google; it learns that the teacher signed in to FullVision, and nothing about their classes or students.

FullVision does not give information to anyone else unless the law requires it.

How long it is kept

  • Information stays until the teacher deletes it.
  • Deleting a class hides it right away and permanently deletes it, with its students' records, after 30 days. Until then, it can be restored by contacting the operator.
  • Deleting your account: contact the operator, who deletes it for you. Everything you stored is then permanently deleted after 30 days.
  • A student who no longer belongs to any class is deleted along with that class.
  • Change history for scores and term ratings is kept for 2 years, or until its class is deleted.
  • A student's cards, and what is recorded about them, are deleted with the student or with their class. When a student opened a piece of work is deleted with that piece or that student.
  • The record of tries at slip and card codes is kept for 30 days.
  • Backups. Supabase makes a daily backup of the database and keeps each one for 7 days. A deleted item can remain in backups for up to 7 days after it is deleted.

How it is protected

  • All connections use HTTPS encryption.
  • Supabase encrypts the stored data and backups.
  • Teachers' passwords are stored only as one-way hashes and are never visible to the operator.
  • Database rules restrict every request to the signed-in teacher's own data.
  • Student card codes and passwords are kept only as keyed fingerprints, made with a key the database never holds. Slip codes are stored as hashes, and slip passwords as printed. Every code is blocked after repeated wrong tries.
  • The pages only run scripts from FullVision itself and from Sentry's error-reporting service.

If something goes wrong

If information in FullVision is lost, or seen by someone who should not see it, the operator will tell the affected teachers as soon as possible and say what happened, what information was involved, and what is being done. Teachers can then inform their school or district as their own policies require.

Your choices

  • Teachers can view, correct and delete their information and their students' information at any time in the app, and can delete their account.
  • Students and parents who want to see, correct or remove information should ask the teacher or school, who control the records. The operator will help a teacher respond to a request.
  • For any other privacy question or request, email colin.brown04@gmail.com.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes too. Changes that affect how student information is stored or shared will be sent to teachers by email before they take effect.
