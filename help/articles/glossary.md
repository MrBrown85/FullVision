### Average of Percentages

A category calculation that turns each assessment into a percent first, so each counts the same however many points it has. Factor still counts an assessment more than once.

### BC Letter Bands

The BC letter grades and the percent each starts at: A 86%, B 73%, C+ 67%, C 60% and C− 50%, with anything under 50% shown as IE until the last reporting term ends, then F. The BC Letter Bands button on the Grading tab sets every Counts As value, with its + and −, to match them.

### Calculation Method

The rule that turns a student's scores on one competency into a single proficiency level, set on the Grading tab; new classes start on Mean. Every method skips work that has not been scored.

### Categories

Weighted groups of assessments in the course %, set under Course Percentage; the weights must total 100%. A category with no marked work for a student is left out, and the other categories share its weight.

### Core Competencies

The seven BC Core Competencies, in three groups: Communication, Thinking, and Personal & Social. An assessment can be tagged with the ones it draws on, but they do not count toward any grade.

### Count Only the Most Recent

A Grading tab setting that counts only each competency's last 3 or 5 scores by date; the default is All Scores. It applies to every Calculation Method except Most Recent Score and does not change the course %.

### Counts As

The percent each proficiency level, with its + and −, counts as in the course % of a Letter or Both class, set under Points and Proficiency on the Grading tab. The default is the level ÷ 4 (Extending 100%, Proficient 75%, Developing 50%, Emerging 25%); new grade 10–12 classes start on the BC Letter Bands.

### Course Percentage

The course % of a Letter or Both class, and the Grading tab section that sets how it comes from points and proficiency. With no categories, it is total points earned out of total points possible, and the letter grade comes from the % as shown.

### Curricular Competencies

What students are expected to do in a BC course, in the Ministry's own words; a class takes its competencies from its courses in the BC curriculum. Scores on them make up each student's competency proficiency.

### Decay Weight

The share each newer score carries in a Decaying Average, from 10% to 95%; the default is 65%. A higher value makes recent scores count more.

### Decaying Average

A Calculation Method in which every score counts and newer ones count more. Starting from the oldest score, each newer score moves the average: new average = old average × (100% − Decay Weight) + new score × Decay Weight. An assessment with a higher Factor moves it more.

### Developing

The second of the four BC proficiency levels, scored as 2: a partial understanding of the concepts and competencies relevant to the expected learning.

### Emerging

The first of the four BC proficiency levels, scored as 1: an initial understanding of the concepts and competencies relevant to the expected learning.

### Excused

A status for work a student does not have to do. Excused work is left out of every grade.

### Extending

The highest of the four BC proficiency levels, scored as 4: a sophisticated understanding of the concepts and competencies relevant to the expected learning.

### Factor

An assessment's weight, chosen on the assessment form from 0.5x to 5x; the default is 1x (Normal). At 2x an assessment counts twice as much as work at 1x in the course % and under Mean, Mode and Decaying Average; Highest Score, Median and Most Recent Score ignore it.

### Folders Count as One

A Grading tab switch, on for a one-course class and off for a combined class: each folder of competencies is averaged first and counts as one competency in the overall proficiency. When it is off, every competency counts the same and folders only group them.

### Grading System

The class setting that decides how the grade is shown: Proficiency (a level from Emerging to Extending, out of 4), Letter (a letter grade and %), or Both (the level and the letter grade and %, side by side). New grade 10–12 classes start on Letter, and other grades on Proficiency.

### Highest Score

A Calculation Method in which the highest score counts, whenever it was earned.

### How Much Newer Work Counts

The Grading tab's name for the Decay Weight.

### I Can Statements

First-person statements of a curricular competency, each beginning "I can", written for every competency in the BC curriculum. They can be edited, added and deleted for each competency in Class Settings, and printed reports can show them.

### IE

Insufficient Evidence. In a Proficiency or Both class, the roster code for a student with no scored work yet. In a Letter or Both class, IE replaces the letter grade for a course % under 50% until the last reporting term ends, then F.

### Late

A status a teacher marks on work handed in late; the score counts as usual.

### Mean

A Calculation Method that adds the scores and divides by how many there are. An assessment with a higher Factor counts more.

### Median

A Calculation Method that sorts the scores and takes the middle one. With an even number of scores, it takes the halfway point between the two middle scores.

### Missing

A status for work that was not handed in; work is Missing only when it is marked Missing, whatever its due date. It counts as 0 in the course % and is left out of competency proficiency as no evidence.

### Mode

A Calculation Method that takes the level that comes up most, counted by whole level, so 3+ and 3 both count as 3. A tie goes to the most recent of the tied scores, keeping its + or −.

### Most Recent Score

A Calculation Method in which only the score with the latest date counts.

### Plus (+) and Minus (−)

A + or − on a proficiency level is worth a third of a level, so 3+ counts as 3.33 and 3− as 2.67. Every level can take one, except that there is no 1− and no 4+.

### Proficient

The third of the four BC proficiency levels, scored as 3: a complete understanding of the concepts and competencies relevant to the expected learning.

### Reporting Terms

A class's report dates, up to six, each with a label, a start date and an end date, set on the Reporting Terms tab in Class Settings. Grades build all year: a report shows the year to date as of its term's end.

### Total Points

A category calculation, and the default, that adds up every assessment's points, so work worth more points, such as a rubric with more criteria, counts more.
