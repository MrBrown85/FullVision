# Holding the device write of a score: design

Status, 2026-11-13. **A design only. Nothing here is built, and merging this document approves nothing.**

- **Asked for:** Colin ruled on 2026-11-13 that the whole-class write to this device may be held for about a third of a second after a score click and written when the tab hides or closes. His conditions: the outbox is still written at once on every click, shown by a test; an independent reviewer reads the design before any code; no mark may ever be lost.
- **This document's answer:** the hold can be made safe, and section 5 says how. **It recommends not building it now.** After the change in "the rule on the changed cell" (the PR beside this one), the write that would be held costs under a millisecond a click on an ordinary computer. The hold would add a stretch in which this device's copy is behind, a dozen places that must flush or drop what is held, and one way to put a student's marks back on a shared computer after Sign Out. Section 10 has the numbers and what to build instead if the write ever shows in a measurement.
- **Why a new file.** [`one-mark-save-design.md`](one-mark-save-design.md) is kept as the record of step 4 and is closed. Its "Part B" is a different list. This document is the held write only.

Every function is named, not numbered: find each by name on `origin/main` (`f6a99777`) with the changed-cell PR on top. What this document says of today's code was traced by reading it, not run, unless it is marked **measured** (run for this document; section 10 says how) or **reproduced** (a test in the changed-cell PR shows it).

Words, as in the record: a **mark** is a score a teacher gives. A **cell** is one student on one piece of work. The **saved copy** is the class's marks kept in the browser under one key, `gb-scores-<class id>`, about 0.5 to 1.4 MB for a class in November. The **outbox** is the list of calls waiting to go to the server, kept under `fv-sync-queue-v1` (`shared/offline-queue.js`). The **rows** are the Draft and Returned rows, `gb-grade-returns-<class id>`. A **one-cell write** is a level, a + or −, or one of the three comments. A **whole-list save** is `saveScores`: points, Complete and Incomplete, Clear Score, every Undo, group copies, Delete Assessment.

## 1. What the design record says

Nothing in `one-mark-save-design.md` or `DECISIONS.md` rules a held write out, and neither mentions one. Three things in them bind it.

1. **The flush must write this tab's whole memory, as today.** The record recommends against a tab writing only its own cells (step 10b: "Recommendation: do not build 10b, 11 or 12 now"), and step 4 depends on it: "If a later step ever writes anything else to storage (Part B, step 10b), the kept text stays this tab's memory." A flush that patched a few cells into whatever the saved copy holds would be step 10b by another door.
2. **Sync failures show only on the sync dot** (`CLAUDE.md` rule 5). A held write that fails cannot answer `false` to the click that made it. The dot already shows a full storage (`getSaveProblem`), a third of a second later here.
3. **"Still needs Colin: changing how a mark is saved, queued, synced, returned or deleted"** (`DECISIONS.md`, "Marks may be moved"). This is such a change. The record's method for it stands: tests first, an independent reviewer who tries to break it, undone by reverting one PR.

The record also measured the thing this would speed up: "The save itself, one mark, 5,400-mark class: 1.6 ms (4.0 ms at quarter speed)", and concluded "the save is already fast. If a teacher feels a wait, it is the redraw on the desktop." Section 10 measures it again and agrees.

## 2. What one score click writes today, in order

A level on plain work, signed in, the student page on, through `upsertScore` → `_upsertScoreOne`. Everything below happens before the click's handler returns. What is written and sent, and the order of the calls, is pinned by `tests/pin-*.test.js`.

| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |

| 2   | The saved copy is read as text and compared with the text this tab last wrote. It is not parsed.                                                              | Reads `gb-scores-<cid>`                                | `_gradeBefore`, `_savedScoresAreMine`                                    |
| 3   | The whole class's scores are turned into text and written.                                                                                                    | **`gb-scores-<cid>`**                                  | `_safeLSSet`; it also keeps the text as this tab's own copy (step 4)     |
| 4   | The Draft and Returned rule runs on the cell. If the grade changed, the class's rows are written and other tabs are told.                                     | Memory, **`gb-grade-returns-<cid>`**, BroadcastChannel | `_gradeAfter`, `_gradeRule`, `_saveGradeReturns`                         |

The three comment writers are the same with another call. In Demo Mode steps 6 to 8 do nothing: there is no outbox and no server.

Two things about today's order matter to this design.

- **The outbox is written last of the three.** Between step 3 and step 6 the mark is in the saved copy and not in the outbox. It is a moment of script, well under a millisecond, but, as the code reads (this was not run), a crash in it leaves a mark that shows on this device and is never sent: the next whole-list save compares with this tab's own copy, which holds the mark, so it sends nothing for it, and the next class load takes it away. The hold would turn this round, and that is to its credit: the outbox would be the first thing stored.
- **The edited flag is on disk before any mark can be only in memory.** It is written at the first edit after a full load and stays until the next full load (`_bundleToCache` is the only place that clears it). Section 6 leans on this.

## 3. What must stay at once, and why

| Step                                  | Hold it? | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------- | -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Memory                             | No       | The screen draws from it.                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| 4. The rule and its rows              | No       | The rule needs the cell as it was, which only this moment has. The rows are small. Section 6 says what a crash leaves when the rows are ahead of the saved copy.                                                           |
| 5. The edited flag                    | **No**   | It is what makes the next page load fetch the class in full instead of trusting a saved copy that is behind.                                                                                                               |
| 6. The outbox                         | **No**   | Colin's condition, and the whole argument: it is the only thing that gets a mark to the server. It is written inside `enqueue`, in the same script as the click, and this design does not touch `shared/offline-queue.js`. |
| 7. The answer when the outbox refuses | No       | The page and the baseline act on it at once.                                                                                                                                                                               |
| 8. The baseline                       | No       | A whole-list save a moment later works out what to send from it.                                                                                                                                                           |
| 3. The saved copy                     | Can be   | Nothing reads it in the same step. What reads it later is section 4.                                                                                                                                                       |
| 9. Telling other tabs                 | Must be  | Another tab that hears "scores changed" may read the saved copy at once. The message must follow the write it announces, so it moves to the flush.                                                                         |

**The rule that makes "no mark is lost" true.** At every moment a mark is in at least one of three places: the saved copy, the outbox, or the server. So the write of a mark may be held **only when the outbox has just taken its call**. If the call did not go into the outbox, the saved copy is the mark's only home and it is written at once, as today:

- Demo Mode (no outbox, and a class switch or another tab's message re-reads the class from the saved copy over memory: `_doInitData` ends in `_loadCourseFromLS`);
- a class, student or piece of work whose id was made on this device and has no server id yet (no call is sent, and when the class gets its server id `_moveCourseDataLocalToCanonical` replays the class **from the saved copy's text**: a copy that is behind there means marks that never reach the server);
- a call the outbox refused (fault 10 in the record: the mark lives only on this device already);
- any failure to tell which of these it is.

## 5. The proposal, made exact

Two forms were weighed. They differ in what is done at the click.

- **Form 1, hold the write only.** At the click the class's scores are still turned into text. Only `localStorage.setItem` waits. Every reader in this tab is handed the held text in place of the saved copy, so every one of them sees, letter for letter, what it sees today.
- **Form 2, hold the text too.** Nothing is made at the click. The flush turns memory into text when it runs. It saves about twice as much (section 10). It has a trap that Form 1 does not, and no clean way round it:

  > Screens change the class's scores in memory **before** they call a whole-list save: Cmd+Z, Clear Score, Delete Assessment and the phone's Undo all take `getScores()`, change it in place, and hand it to `saveScores`, which reads the saved copy to learn what the class held before. With a write held, the saved copy is behind. If `saveScores` "just flushes first", the flush takes its text from memory, which already holds the screen's change. The save then sees no change at all: the Draft and Returned rule does not run, the guard has nothing to judge, a group partner is not copied, and a tab with no baseline sends nothing, so **the mark never reaches the server**. Nothing fails and nothing is shown.

  Form 2 could be made right only by also keeping every entry written since the last flush and laying them over the saved copy for each reader. That is a second picture of the class, kept in step by hand, in the code the record calls the riskiest in the app.

**This design is Form 1.** Its rules:

1. **What is held.** The `setItem` of `gb-scores-<cid>` made by a one-cell score writer, and the BroadcastChannel message that follows it. Nothing else. The text is made where it is made today and becomes this tab's own copy at once (step 4's `_keepText`). Steps 1, 2 and 4 to 8 of section 2 run at once, in today's order. Whether the write waits is decided when the outbox has answered (step 6), a few lines later: if it does not wait, it is made then.
2. **When.** Only when all of these hold; otherwise the write is made at once, as today:
   - the writer handed at least one call to the outbox in this step and the outbox took every call it was handed;
   - the class's own id, the student's and the work's are server ids;
   - the saved copy is this tab's own at the click (`_savedScoresAreMine`'s check of the text). A tab that is already behind another writes at once, as today, so rule 5 can tell a write made after the click from one made before it;
   - the write is not inside a group copy and the cell is not pair or group work (`_groupWriteDepth`, `isLinkedGroupMember`). Rule 6 would keep those right, but a whole-list save follows in the same step and writes the saved copy anyway, so there is nothing to gain;
   - the hold is switched on (rule 10).
3. **What is kept while held**, for each class, in memory only: the text (the same string step 4 keeps as this tab's own copy, so nothing is copied), the text last written to storage, and whether other tabs are to be told.
4. **How long.** One timer for each class, set when the first write is held and **not pushed back** by later ones: 330 ms. A teacher who types for a minute still has a saved copy at most a third of a second behind.
5. **The flush** writes the held text through `_safeLSSet`, then tells other tabs once, then forgets the held text. First it reads the saved copy: if that is no longer the text this tab last wrote, another tab has written since the click. Today that tab's write would have replaced this one, so the held text is dropped, not written. A flush that fails (storage full) is today's failed write: the sync dot says so, and `_scoresApart` is set so the next save compares the whole class.
6. **Readers in this tab read through the held text.** One function answers "the saved copy's text": the held text when there is one, else `localStorage.getItem`. `_gradeBefore`, `_loadPersistedCourseFieldSnapshot` for scores, and `_savedScoresAreMine` (against the text last written) use it. So a whole-list save a moment after a held click reads exactly what it reads today, and the trap above cannot open: the text was made at the click, before any screen touched memory again.
7. **Any other write of the key drops the held text**, in `_safeLSSet` itself: a whole-list save, a class load, a re-key. So does a class load that finds the saved text unchanged and writes nothing (`_persistLoadedField`). The newer text is the truth, and the older one must not land on top of it.
8. **Removing the key drops the held text, and the flush checks before it writes.** Sign Out and every remover in section 4 call one "drop everything held" function first. As a second lock the flush writes nothing unless the class is still in memory and this page has not wiped the device.
9. **What flushes besides the timer:**
   - `visibilitychange` to hidden: the last moment a phone or a backgrounded tab can be relied on to give;
   - `pagehide`: a reload, a closed tab, a link away, the reload for a new version (`shared/auto-update.js`). Registered when `shared/data.js` loads, so it runs before the pages' own `pagehide` saves and before a sign-out's last wipe (`_onSignedOutElsewhere`);
   - `freeze` and `beforeunload`, which cost nothing where they do nothing;
   - a class switch (`switchActiveCourse`), so nothing is held for a class that is no longer on screen and could be evicted;
   - going offline is not one: nothing about the hold depends on the connection.
10. **A switch.** `FV_HOLD_SCORE_WRITE` in `constants.js`, like `FV_CACHE_FIRST`. Off, every write is made at the click and none of this runs.

What the hold does **not** change: what is sent, when, or in what order; the rows; the baseline; what a click answers, except that a full storage is learned a third of a second later.

## 7. Where it could lose data

Plainly, in order of how bad.

1. **A student's marks put back on a shared computer after Sign Out.** `signOut` wipes the device and then waits, for up to two seconds and more, for the report copies and the photo queue to be deleted before it leaves the page. A timer set just before would fire in that wait and write the class back, and this tab has no second wipe on `pagehide`. Nothing is lost; something is left behind that must not be (FOIPPA). The same shape leaves a scores key with no record beside it after Delete Class, the 14-day purge or an eviction, and no purge ever finds such a key. Rule 8 closes it twice over, and it is the first thing a reviewer should try to break.
2. **A mark held that is not in the outbox.** Then a crash loses it for good, and for a class made on this device the move to its server id replays a copy that is behind. Rule 2 forbids it. The danger is a later change that adds a way to save a score and holds it without the outbox's answer in hand.
3. **Form 2's trap** (section 5): a flush from memory in the middle of a whole-list save. A mark is not sent, with no error. Form 1 does not have it. It is listed because the first idea anyone has for speeding this up further is "do not make the text at the click either".
4. **A crash while offline.** No mark is lost, but the teacher sees the old score for as long as the device is offline, on a class they were just grading, and may not trust what they see.
5. **Two tabs.** The stretch in which a second tab can start behind grows by a third of a second. The server is not affected.

Not a loss, but a change someone will notice: six of the `tests/pin-*.test.js` files read the saved scores straight after a write (about 40 assertions). Those that follow a one-cell write would have to move their check to after a flush. The pins record exactly the thing this changes.

## 8. Tests that would prove it

Written first, and failing before the build where they can. The first is Colin's condition.

1. **The outbox is written at once.** With the real outbox, one level click: before any timer has run, `fv-sync-queue-v1` holds the call with its contents, and `gb-scores-<cid>` holds the old text. The same for a + or −, each comment, and ten clicks in a row (ten calls or their replacements, one old text).
2. **Not held** when: Demo Mode; a class, student or piece with a local id; the outbox full; storage too full for the outbox; pair work; a member's comment cleared inside a group copy; the switch off. In each the saved copy holds the mark when the click returns.
3. **The timer.** Ten clicks 20 ms apart: one write of the key, 330 ms after the first click, and its text is the text of memory.
4. **Each flush event** on its own writes at once: `visibilitychange` to hidden, `pagehide`, `freeze`, `beforeunload`, a class switch.
5. **The same class, played both ways** (the method of `tests/grade-rule-one-cell.test.js`): every scenario there with the hold on and off, compared after every step once the clock has moved on: the same saved text, rows and calls. Then again with a whole-list save in the same tick as a held click: a level then Cmd+Z, then points, then Clear Score, then Delete Assessment, then Undo of a fill. Then a long run in no chosen order.
6. **A crash.** A click, then a new page with no flush: the class has no baseline, the outbox still holds the call, the fake server answers the class with the mark once the call has landed, and the screen shows it. The same with the edited flag's write refused.
7. **Nothing comes back.** A held click, then each of: `signOut` (with its real waits), Delete Class, `_purgeStaleSavedCopies`, `_purgeUnlistedCourseData`, `_evictOldestSavedCopy`, a class moved to its server id. Run every timer after: no `gb-scores-` key for that class exists.
8. **A newer write wins.** A held click, then a class load that replaces the scores, then every timer: the saved copy is the server's. The same with a re-key.
9. **Two tabs**, in the browser (as `e2e/two-tabs-behind-keeps-marks.spec.js`): a click in one, the second opened inside the third of a second, offline; then online. Both show the mark, and the server was sent it once. And: a second tab that saves inside the stretch; the first tab's text is dropped; a whole-list save in either clears nothing.
10. **Other tabs are told once, after the write**, and not at all for a call the outbox refused.
11. **Storage full at the flush:** the dot is red, and the next click compares the whole class.
12. **The pins**, changed to check after a flush, with the PR listing each changed line. `tests/speed-guard.test.js`: "saved" for one click falls by the size of the saved copy, and a new step, ten clicks, counts one write of it.
13. **The differential run** (`docs/differential-run.md`), three seeds, both modes, offline and two tabs, 0 differences. It moves the clock six seconds a step, so every hold has flushed when it compares. A second run that moves the clock 100 ms after a step shows the stretch itself and names each key that differs: only `gb-scores-…`, and only until the next move.

The run matters more than it looks. For the changed-cell PR it found a case that reading the code twice and 2,238 compared steps in vitest had missed.

## 9. Undo

Set `FV_HOLD_SCORE_WRITE` to false, or revert the one PR. Nothing is stored in a new shape: the same key, the same text, later. A page running the old code and a page running the new one can share a browser; the old one just never holds.

## 10. What it would buy, and the recommendation

**Measured** for this document, in Chromium through Playwright, on a class of 30 students with two entries a cell. Median of 40. The computer was busy with other work, so read these as rough.

| Class              | Speed   | Text made at the click (`JSON.stringify`) | The write (`setItem`) | Read and compare the saved text (the changed-cell check) |
| ------------------ | ------- | ----------------------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| 36 pieces, 0.66 MB | Full    | 0.5 ms                                    | 0.3 ms                | 0.3 ms                                                   |
| 36 pieces, 0.66 MB | Quarter | 2.1 ms                                    | 1.2 ms                | 1.1 ms                                                   |
| 75 pieces, 1.37 MB | Full    | 1.1 ms                                    | 0.8 ms                | 0.6 ms                                                   |
| 75 pieces, 1.37 MB | Quarter | 4.3 ms                                    | 4.0 ms                | 2.4 ms                                                   |

And the whole save for one level click, timed in Node (the same JavaScript engine, no real storage): 3.5 ms before the changed-cell PR and 0.3 ms after, on 0.5 MB; 7.8 ms and 0.6 ms on 1.06 MB.

So:

- **Form 1 saves the write: 0.3 to 0.8 ms a click, 1.2 to 4.0 ms on the slow machine.**
- Form 2 would save the text as well: 0.8 to 1.9 ms, 3.3 to 8.3 ms on the slow machine.
- **It moves the write more than it removes it.** A teacher who clicks slower than three times a second still gets one write for each click, a third of a second later, in the pause. Writes are only saved outright when marks come faster than that: fast typing down a column, and fills.
- The same click spends 200 to 400 ms redrawing (findings 1 to 3 of [`speed-assessment.md`](speed-assessment.md)). A teacher cannot feel one millisecond in three hundred.

**Recommendation: do not build the hold now.** What it would buy is at the edge of what can be measured, and what it would cost is a stretch in which this device's copy is not the truth, in the one piece of code where the record has twice found a design worse than what it replaced.

The lines below are my reading of each one's published work. None of them has seen this design.

- **Beck** (_Tidy First?_): do not pay for a change before there is a reason to. The reason was a guess (finding 4 lists it beside two fixes that did matter); the measurement does not bear it out.
- **Nygard** (_Release It!_): do not put a new way to fail on the busiest path without need. This is the busiest path, and section 7 lists five new ways.
- **Kleppmann** (_Designing Data-Intensive Applications_, on write-ahead logs): the design is sound in his terms. The outbox is the log, written first; the saved copy is a snapshot that may lag. He would also say a snapshot that lags needs a recovery path that is exercised, not argued, and today's is a full download on a flag.
- **The privacy commissioner's office**: a write that can fire after Sign Out is a new place for a student's marks to outlive the session on a shared computer. It is closed in the design, and it would not exist without the hold.

**If the device write ever shows in a measurement, build this first:** one write for each action, not for each cell. Set Everyone To, a row or column fill and Set Unmarked To call a one-cell writer once for each student, so a fill of 30 writes the whole class 30 times, and reads it back 30 times for the changed-cell check: 33 to 75 ms on an ordinary computer and 130 to 320 ms on the slow one, all in one click. Holding those writes until the fill's own loop ends, in the same script, makes it one. There is no timer, no stretch another tab or a crash can fall into, nothing to flush when the tab hides, and nothing that can fire after Sign Out. It needs rule 6 (a group member inside a fill is followed by a whole-list save) and tests 5 and 12, and nothing else from this document. It is still a change to how a mark is saved, so it still needs Colin and a reviewer.

## 11. Questions for the reviewer and for Colin

1. **Is the recommendation right?** If Colin wants the hold built anyway, it should be Form 1, with every rule in section 5, and test 1 written first.
2. **Should the rows be held with the scores?** This design keeps them at once, as the smaller change. After a crash the rows can then be ahead of the saved copy until the class is read again: a Draft badge on a score that shows its old value. Holding both would keep the two in step and adds a second key to every table above.
3. **Is 330 ms from the first click right, or should it be shorter?** For a teacher clicking, 100 ms moves the same write off the click, and the stretch a crash can fall into is a third as long. Only fast typing and fills gain from a longer hold, and a fill is better served by the one-write-per-action change.
4. **`signOut` has no second wipe on `pagehide` in the tab that signs out.** Other tabs do (`_onSignedOutElsewhere`). That is worth a look whether or not this is built: a page's own `pagehide` save after the wipe would be left behind today.
