# Splash page brief (2026-11-11)

What the page at fullvision.ca has to say, and to whom. Written for the Nov 11 redesign, which brings back the Nov 3 layout (two columns, fact cards, phones beside screens) with less text than it had, and fresh pictures from Demo Mode (`scripts/splash-shots.mjs`).

## Who reads it

1. **A BC teacher** who heard about it from a colleague. Has five minutes and a Learning Update due.
2. **A principal or the superintendent** a teacher forwards it to, asking "can I use this?"
3. **A district privacy or IT officer** asked to sign off.

The page answers the teacher first and the other two in one section of their own (For Schools).

## What the council would say

The council is the panel named in DECISIONS ("Student page build rulings", Nov 10). These are the voices that bear on a landing page.

- **Krug:** A visitor must know in five seconds what this is, who it is for and what to do next. One headline, one sentence, one button. Real screenshots, not drawings. Don't make them read.
- **Nielsen:** People scan in an F. Put the payoff in the first two words of each heading. Show what the screen does, not a feature list. Credibility comes from specifics: real numbers (226 courses, 5,029 competencies), a real name, a privacy page.
- **Guskey:** Lead with the purpose of grading: to tell students and families clearly where the learner is. Show the report a parent reads, and that it is about learning, not behaviour points.
- **O'Connor:** Show that the grade follows the evidence: most recent work, Missing that counts no level, behaviour kept out of the grade. Don't promise averages-of-everything.
- **Wiliam:** Feedback should be more work for the student than for the teacher. The strongest thing to show is the loop: work goes in, feedback comes back, the student acts on it (Reflect on Feedback, Return for Revision, Before and After).
- **The Ministry's reporting policy:** Name the K–12 Student Reporting Policy's parts and show each one: the Proficiency Scale, descriptive feedback, student self-assessment of the Core Competencies, goal setting, and Learning Updates through the year.
- **The privacy commissioner's office (OIPC BC):** Say where the data is (Canada), who sees it (the teacher), what is not collected (no student accounts, no email), what is deleted and when (photos, on a date the teacher sets), and that nothing goes to an AI service. Plainly, near the top, not only in a policy.
- **OWASP:** Don't claim more than is built. No "bank-level security"; say what is actually done.

## What the superintendent wants to see

Surrey is the largest district in BC. A superintendent judging a tool asks:

1. **Does it follow the reporting policy?** Proficiency Scale K–9, letter grades and percent 10–12, descriptive feedback, Core Competency self-assessment and goals. A checklist they can tick.
2. **Is student data safe, and in Canada?** FOIPPA, stored in Canada, teacher-only access, no AI, students without accounts, photos deleted on schedule, a privacy page a privacy officer can read.
3. **Does it cut teacher workload?** Report season is the pain point. Comments from evidence, report cards that fill themselves, grading on a phone.
4. **Does it help every learner?** Designations on the roster, students hearing feedback and acting on it, families reading a clear report.
5. **Does it work with what we have?** MyEducation BC class lists import as they are; reports print on Letter or Legal, black and white.
6. **Can a teacher leave with their data?** Class Backup.

## What only FullVision does

Compared with MyEducation BC's gradebook, Teams Assignments, Schoology, Google Classroom and Spaces:

1. **The BC curriculum, word for word, built in.** 226 courses, 5,029 competencies, with I Can statements, picked in the New Class wizard.
2. **Comments written from the evidence, with no AI.** The questionnaire puts the student's year, observations and own words beside a Comment Bank in BC's four parts; Generate assembles your own sentences.
3. **Students without accounts.** A printed card opens a student's page: questionnaires, self-assessment, goals, sending work and reading feedback. No student email, no password.
4. **Paper work, graded on screen.** A student photographs the page; the teacher ticks the rubric beside it, marks it up, and returns it. The Advanced Rubric suggests the level; the teacher decides.
5. **Feedback the student answers.** Reflect on Feedback: Did it make sense? A question for the teacher, a plan for next time, and "Did you do it?" on the next piece. Questions land on the teacher's dashboard.
6. **A gradebook that reads like the reporting policy.** Proficiency with + and −, Letter, or Both; grades build all year; Missing counts no level.
7. **Report cards that fill themselves.** Page Studio blocks fill from the gradebook for every student: Report Card, Interim Update, Conference Pack, Quick Report.
8. **The class on a phone, offline.** Grade and observe from a phone; marks send when the Wi-Fi is back.
